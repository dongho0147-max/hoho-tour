Sokcho is the best one-night escape from Seoul if you want a mountain, a beach, and a working fishing port in a single trip. You leave Seoul in the morning, you are on a cable car above Seoraksan by the afternoon, and you eat dinner next to fishing boats while the sun goes down over the East Sea. One night is enough. Two would be better, but one works, and this plan shows you how.

This guide is written from research, not from a single visit. Routes, times, and prices below come from recent listings and reviews, checked November 2026. Treat prices as ranges, not promises, and check the bus timetable in Naver Map before you go.

## Should you take the bus or a tour?

Here is the honest answer: the bus is cheaper and more flexible, and the tour is easier. Express buses run regularly from Seoul Express Bus Terminal to Sokcho, and the ride takes about two and a half hours each way. You pick your own pace, you stay for sunset, and you sleep in Sokcho instead of rushing back. The trade-off is that you handle every connection yourself, including the local bus from Sokcho to the national park.

A guided day tour from Seoul does the driving for you. The Seoraksan and Naksansa Temple full-day tour on Klook runs about 12.5 hours, from 07:20 to 19:50, with an English and Chinese speaking guide, and recent listings rate it 4.7 out of 5 from more than 1,100 reviews. Read the fine print before you book: on that listing the cable car is not included, so budget for it separately if you want the ride up.

> **Tip:** If you go by bus, leave Seoul early. The mountain deserves your morning energy, and the beach deserves your evening. A late start squeezes both, and Sokcho traffic on weekend afternoons is slow.

## What does the mountain day actually look like?

Start at Seoraksan National Park, at the Sogongwon entrance on the Sokcho side. The park is huge, so do not try to see all of it. Pick one route and walk it properly. The most popular first-timer route is the cable car up to Gwongeumseong Fortress, a short walk to the viewpoint, and then back down to the valley floor for Sinheungsa Temple.

The cable car is the part people remember. It lifts you over the valley in a few minutes, and the granite peaks open up below you. Go early if you can, because the queue grows fast on clear weekends, and the car can pause in strong wind. If the cable car is closed for weather, the valley walk to Sinheungsa is still worth your time. The temple sits in a quiet clearing with a large bronze Buddha, and the stream beside the path is the kind of place that slows you down without trying.

## How hard is the hiking, really?

Ulsanbawi Rock is the famous hard hike, and you should know what you are signing up for. It is a steep stone-step climb of about 3.8 km one way, and most people take two to three hours up and less coming down. The view from the top is superb, but it is a real workout, and it eats half a day. If you only have one night, skip it and take the cable car instead.

The valley trails near the entrance are the opposite: flat, shaded, and easy for anyone in walking shoes. That is the right call for a short trip. Bring water, wear shoes with grip, and dress in layers. The mountain is noticeably colder and windier than Sokcho town, even on a mild day, and the weather can turn while you are up there.

## Where do you sleep, and what do you eat?

Sleep in Sokcho town, near the beach or the port, not up by the park entrance. The town is small, the bus connections are simple, and everything you want in the evening is within a short taxi ride. Guesthouses and small hotels cluster near Sokcho Beach, and larger hotels sit along the seafront. If you are staying overnight, it is worth comparing stays before you go, since weekend rooms near the beach sell out first.

Dinner belongs to Daepohang Port. The port is a working harbor with a long row of sashimi restaurants and dried-fish stalls, and it stays lively into the evening. Order a sashimi set if you eat raw fish, or go for grilled fish and a hot soup if you do not. A full dinner for two at the port usually lands in the 40,000-80,000 KRW range based on recent listings, depending on what you order. If raw fish is not your thing, Sokcho has two local dishes worth trying instead: dakgangjeong, the sweet and crispy fried chicken sold by weight in the market, and squid sundae, a Sokcho specialty stuffed with squid and vegetables.

## What about Abai Village and the beach?

Save the morning of day two for the coast. Sokcho Beach is a long, clean stretch of sand with a promenade, and it is at its best in the early morning before the crowds arrive. Walk it, then cross to Abai Village, the small neighborhood on the other side of the harbor. Abai Village was settled by families who came from the north during the Korean War, and it still feels different from the rest of town: narrow lanes, low houses, and small restaurants serving Abai sundae.

The Gaetbae boat, a small hand-pulled ferry across the harbor channel, is the charming way to get there. It is a short ride and costs a small fare, usually around 500-1,000 KRW based on recent listings. If the boat is not running, the road bridge works fine and the walk is short.

## Skip this if...

Skip the overnight plan if you only want the mountain and nothing else. A day tour from Seoul covers Seoraksan and Naksansa in one long day, and you sleep in your own bed. Skip Ulsanbawi if your knees complain on stairs, if the forecast is wet, or if you arrive after midday. And skip Daepohang if you want a quiet dinner, because the port is busy, loud, and full of hawkers, and that is exactly the point of it.
