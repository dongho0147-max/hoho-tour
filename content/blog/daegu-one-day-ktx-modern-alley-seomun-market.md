Daegu is the easiest big-city day trip in Korea that most travelers skip. The KTX from Seoul takes under two hours, the subway costs a flat {{< pr "1,500 KRW" >}} a ride, and the center packs a century of modern history, one of the country's oldest markets, and a mountain view into a single walkable day. Come hungry: Daegu has its own list of ten official local dishes, and several of them are things you cannot get done properly anywhere else.

This guide is written from research, not from a single visit. Times and prices below come from official city and operator pages, checked November 19, 2026. Anything we could not confirm against an official page is marked (미확인, unverified), so treat those lines as leads, not promises.

## 🚄 How do you get to Daegu?

By KTX from Seoul Station to Dongdaegu Station, the trip takes about 1 hour 40 minutes. Since the KTX and SRT systems merged on September 1, 2026, fares vary by train depending on how many stops it makes. A standard one-way adult ticket from Seoul to Dongdaegu runs 32,600 to {{< pr "39,000 KRW" >}}, checked on Korail's own booking site on November 19, 2026. Fares differ by train type and time of day, so the same route can cost quite a bit more or less depending on which departure you pick. Book a few days ahead for weekend mornings.

Dongdaegu Station sits on subway Line 1, and the station building connects straight into the platform. Daegu's subway is flat-fare: {{< pr "1,500 KRW" >}} per ride with a transit card, {{< pr "1,700 KRW" >}} for a single ticket, on all three lines. Lines 1 and 2 cross at Banwoldang, and Line 3 is a monorail that loops through the center and stops right at Seomun Market. Buy a T-money card before you leave Seoul: it works on every Daegu train and bus, but you cannot recharge it in Daegu, so top it up before you board the KTX.

If you would rather have the day organized for you, the Daegu city tour bus leaves from the square in front of Dongdaegu Station. The downtown loop costs {{< pr "10,000 KRW" >}} for adults and {{< pr "8,000 KRW" >}} for teens, with a discount to {{< pr "8,000 KRW" >}} if you hold a same-day train, bus, or plane ticket. It runs Tuesday to Sunday and rests on Mondays, and the operator currently advertises a 30 percent Wednesday discount on its own page. Departure times are hourly from 09:30 to 15:30 in secondary sources, but we could not confirm the timetable on the official page (미확인).

## 🚶 Morning: the Modern Alley walk

Start at Cheongna Hill and walk the Modern Alley course 2, Daegu's signature history walk. The whole loop is 1.64 km and takes about two hours on foot, and it is free and open at all hours. The route strings together the old missionaries' houses on the hill, the March 1st Independence Movement road, Gyesan Cathedral, the former homes of the poets Lee Sang-hwa and Seo Sang-don, and Jin-golmok, the old herbal medicine lane. Free guided tours run on Saturdays at 10:00 and 14:00 from the tourist information center, though the center's own opening hours differ between sources (미확인), so arrive a few minutes early and ask in person.

Gyesan Cathedral is the visual anchor of the walk, a red-brick church from the early 1900s that still holds Mass. The parish office is open 09:00 to 20:00 Tuesday to Friday, 09:00 to 20:30 on Saturdays, and 07:00 to 22:30 on Sundays, with a lunch break from 12:30 to 14:00; it is closed Mondays. The interior viewing hours for visitors are (미확인); only the office hours are officially posted, and access can be limited during Mass, so keep quiet inside and check at the door.

## 🥟 Lunch and afternoon: Seomun Market

Walk ten minutes west to Seomun Market, one of the oldest markets in Korea and still the city's pantry. The day market runs 09:00 to 18:00 and rests on the first and third Sundays of each month, with hours varying stall by stall. Daegu's own tourism office points first-time visitors to three snacks here: kalguksu (knife-cut noodle soup), napjak-mandu (flat pan-fried dumplings), and seed hotteok. Eat them standing up, in that order, from whichever stalls have the local lines.

On Friday, Saturday, and Sunday evenings the market becomes the Seomun Night Market. The 2026 season opened on March 27 and runs through December; the exact closing day in December is (미확인), and the market rests for about three months over winter. Hours are 19:00 to 23:30 on Fridays and Saturdays and 19:00 to 22:30 on Sundays, and it is closed Monday to Thursday.

🔥 Local tip: Ignore any listing that gives night market hours as 17:30 to midnight. That is the old schedule still floating around on the city's own older pages. The current operator and the city's March 27, 2026 notice both say 19:00, and that is the schedule to plan around.

## 🎸 Late afternoon: Kim Gwang-seok Street

Ride or walk east to Kim Gwang-seok Street, a 350-meter mural lane beside Bangcheon Market dedicated to the folk singer who grew up here. It is free, always open, and best taken slowly: the walls carry his lyrics, buskers play his songs on weekends, and the cafes along the lane are the kind you sit in for an hour without meaning to. The nearest station is Kyungpook National University Hospital on Line 2, per secondary sources; the exact walking time from the platform is (미확인), so give yourself fifteen minutes.

## 🚡 Before dark: pick a cable car

Daegu has two cable cars, and one of them fits the end of this day. Apsan Cable Car is the city one, a 15-minute ride from downtown by car. The round trip is {{< pr "14,000 KRW" >}} for adults and {{< pr "10,000 KRW" >}} for children, and the cabin climbs 180 m to 510 m in about 5 minutes. In November the first ride up is 10:30, and the last departure from the top is 18:30 Monday to Thursday and 20:30 on Fridays, weekends, and holidays, with ticket sales closing 30 minutes before the end. On a Friday or weekend evening, the 20:30 last car makes Apsan the best night view in the city.

Palgongsan Cable Car is the mountain one, an hour northeast at 820 m, and it pairs with the temples on Palgongsan rather than the downtown route. The round trip is {{< pr "14,000 KRW" >}} for adults per the city tourism page, and the November hours are 09:30 to 17:10, with seasonal weekend extensions. The operator posts fares and the timetable as images, which we could not read, so both numbers are worth re-checking on the operator's site (미확인) before you build a day around them.

## 🍽️ What to eat: Daegu's official ten

Daegu keeps an official list of ten local dishes, and three of them make a good dinner shortlist for first-timers. Ttaro-gukbap is the city's signature soup, served with the rice and broth in separate bowls, and the city says the chili oil is made by melting beef tallow rather than steeping flakes in vegetable oil, which gives it a deeper, rounder heat. Makchang, grilled beef or pork intestine, is the famous one, and Daegu's tourism office lists dedicated restaurants for it, including Dongdongbae near Namsan-ro and Palgong Makchang off Dongseong-ro. Dongin-dong jjimgalbi is the sleeper pick: a whole alley of spicy braised short rib places on Dongdeok-ro 36-gil, with Wolseong and Dongwon among the listed names.

One honest caveat: opening hours and prices for every one of these restaurants are (미확인). The names and addresses come from the city's official listings, but we could not confirm a single menu price against an official page, so check current hours locally before you cross town for a specific spot.

🔥 Local tip: Do the alley, not the app. For makchang and jjimgalbi both, the city-listed restaurants sit in clusters a few doors apart, and the local habit is to walk the alley once and pick the busiest room. If your first choice has a wait, the place next door is usually just as good.

## 🎡 If you have a second day

E-World and its 83 Tower, the 202 m landmark over Duryu Park, is the add-on for families and night views. We could not confirm any current ticket prices on the official site (미확인); secondary listings put an unlimited ride pass around {{< pr "49,000 KRW" >}} and the tower observatory around 15,000 to {{< pr "18,000 KRW" >}}, so verify before you go. Suseongmot Lake is the quieter option: a 2 km loop around the water, open 24 hours and free, with a music fountain that secondary sources say runs seven times a day (미확인). In spring, the first week of April is cherry blossom season around Duryu Park, Suseongmot, and the Palgongsan ring road; the 2026 bloom opened on March 26.

## 🚫 Skip this if...

Skip the night market plan if you are in Daegu Monday to Thursday, because the night market simply does not open on those days and the day market closes at 18:00. Skip Palgongsan on this itinerary if you also want the alley walk and the market, because the mountain eats half a day and deserves its own. And skip Apsan's late car if the forecast is wet, because the summit view is the whole point.
