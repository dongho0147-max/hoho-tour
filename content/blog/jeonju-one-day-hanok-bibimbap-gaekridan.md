Jeonju is the easiest food city to reach from Seoul, and one day is enough to understand why people love it. You take the KTX in the morning, you walk through the largest hanok village in Korea before lunch, you eat the bibimbap the city is famous for, and you end the afternoon in a cafe on Gaekridan-gil. Then you are back in Seoul for dinner, or you stay the night and see the village lit up. Either way, the day works.

## 🚄 How do you get there?

Take the KTX from Yongsan Station. Trains from Yongsan are far more frequent than from Seoul Station, and the ride to Jeonju Station takes about one hour and forty minutes. Book a round trip as soon as your dates are fixed, because the morning trains out and the evening trains back are the first to fill up on weekends.

From Jeonju Station, take a city bus to the village. Buses 79, 119, 501, 550, and 999 run from the stop in front of the station to the Jeondong Cathedral and [Hanok Village](https://www.klook.com/en-US/activity/2999-jeonju-hanok-village-tour-seoul/?aid=145455) stop in about 25 minutes. Get off there and you are a two or three minute walk from Gyeonggijeon and the main village streets. A taxi from the station costs more but saves you the wait, and it makes sense if you are traveling with luggage or in a group.

> **🔥 Local tip:** Search trains from Yongsan, not Seoul Station. The same line serves both, but Yongsan has far more departures, and the difference on a weekend can be an hour of waiting.

## 🏯 What should you see first?

Start at Gyeonggijeon, the shrine that holds the royal portrait of King Taejo, the founder of the Joseon dynasty. The grounds are calm in the morning, with old trees, a bamboo grove, and the Royal Portrait Museum. One thing to know before you go: the main hall is under dancheong repainting work from March 26 to December 17, 2026, based on recent listings, so you cannot go past the inner gate into the main hall area during that period. The rest of the grounds and the museum stay open as usual, so the visit is still worth it. Just do not plan your morning around a hall you cannot enter.

Entry is a few thousand won per adult. Free guided walks in English run daily at about 11:00 and 15:00 from the ticket office, and they last around an hour. If you arrive early, walk the village streets first and join the later tour.

## 🏘️ How do you walk the village without getting lost in the crowds?

Jeonju Hanok Village has more than 800 hanok houses, and the main streets get crowded by midday, especially on weekends. The trick is to walk the edges. The main road from Gyeonggijeon to Jeondong Catholic Cathedral is the busy spine, and it is worth one walk through. Then turn into the side lanes. The houses are quieter there, the cafes are smaller, and you can actually hear yourself think.

Jeondong Cathedral is worth a stop on the main road. It is a large Romanesque church built in the early 1900s, and its brick front is one of the most photographed corners in the village. Omokdae, a small pavilion on a hill at the edge of the village, is the viewpoint. The climb is short and the view over the rooftops is the best in town, especially in the late afternoon.

Wear shoes you can walk in. The village is all on foot, the lanes are stone and brick, and you will cover several kilometers without noticing.

## 🍚 What to order for lunch

Bibimbap is the dish Jeonju is known for, and the city was named a UNESCO City of Gastronomy in 2012 largely on the strength of it. A proper Jeonju bibimbap comes in a brass bowl with rice, a dozen small toppings, raw beef, and a raw egg yolk, and it costs roughly 12,000-18,000 KRW at the well-known restaurants based on recent listings. The famous places have queues at lunch, so go early or go slightly off-peak. If the queue is an hour long, walk two streets over. The village is full of good kitchens, and the rule that has never failed anyone is to eat where the older locals are eating.

{{< figure src="images/photos/seoul-35.webp" alt="Bibimbap in a bowl with carrot, mushroom, egg, meat and gochujang" caption="The big-name bibimbap houses queue 30 to 40 minutes at weekend lunch. Go early, or walk two streets over. Photo: Chloe Lim, CC BY 2.0" >}}

> **🔥 Local tip:** The big-name bibimbap houses queue 30 to 40 minutes on weekend lunches, and the village default is to wait. Two names come up in nearly every recent roundup, checked November 2026: Hangukjip (1952, third generation, bibimbap 15,000 KRW, yukhoe bibimbap 17,000 KRW, 09:50 to 20:50, one hour of free parking with a meal) and Pungnamjeong (yukhoe bibimbap 13,000 KRW, tteokgalbi 5,000 KRW, break time 15:00 to 16:00). The honest shortcut: go at 11:00 sharp or after 14:00, and order the bibimbap plus tteokgalbi combo. If the line is still an hour, the bowl two streets over is nearly as good and you eat it an hour earlier.

After bibimbap, try the street food. The village lanes sell choco pies, rice cakes, and hotteok from small stalls, and most snacks run 2,000-5,000 KRW each. Buy one thing, walk, then buy the next. That is the whole method, and it works better than sitting down for a second full meal.

> **🔥 Local tip:** The village's other queue is dessert: PNB Pungnyeon Bakery's choco pie and the baguette burger stall on the main street both draw lines, and the southern market's pajeon and makgeolli alley is the local move for the evening. Based on recent visitor guides, checked November 2026.

## ☕️ What is Gaekridan-gil, and why end the day there?

Gaekridan-gil is the cafe street near the old Gaeksa guesthouse, about a ten minute walk from the village. It is where Jeonju goes when it wants coffee instead of history. The street is lined with small cafes, bakeries, and bars in renovated houses, and it is much calmer than the village in the late afternoon. Pick one cafe, sit down, and order something slow. A coffee runs about 5,000-7,000 KRW and a dessert about 6,000-10,000 KRW based on recent listings.

This is also the right place for a hanbok photo if you skipped it in the village. Hanbok rental shops cluster near Gyeonggijeon, with hundreds of outfits and free basic hairstyling, and rental for a few hours usually runs 10,000-20,000 KRW based on recent listings. Wear it in the village in the morning, not in the afternoon, because the light is better and the streets are emptier.

## 🌙 Should you stay the night?

If you can, yes. The village changes after dark, when the day-trippers leave and the lanterns come on. A hanok guesthouse inside the village costs roughly 50,000-120,000 KRW a night based on recent listings, and sleeping on a heated floor in a courtyard house is part of the experience. If you stay, read the companion guide to the village at night for the evening plan.

## 🚫 Skip this if...

Skip the guided tour if you like moving at your own pace, because the KTX and bus route is simple and the village is made for wandering. Skip the famous bibimbap restaurant queue if it is longer than 30 minutes, because the second-best bowl two streets over is nearly as good and you will eat it an hour earlier. And skip the hanbok rental if it is raining, because wet hanok lanes and a rented outfit are a bad combination.
