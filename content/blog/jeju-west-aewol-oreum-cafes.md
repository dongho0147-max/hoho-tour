West Jeju is the slow side of the island. The beaches are paler, the hills are lower, and the day is built around three things: a beach in the morning, tea fields after lunch, and a cafe on the coast at sunset. This route does all three in one day, and it works best if you stop rushing between stops.

This guide was put together from recent listings, reviews, and official pages, checked November 2026. Opening hours and cafe menus change often, so check the big ones the day before you go.

## Start at Hyeopjae Beach and Hallim Park

Start at Hyeopjae Beach, on the northwest coast. It is the pale-sand beach the west is known for, with shallow turquoise water and Biyangdo Island sitting offshore. Come in the morning, when the light is soft and the wind is low. The supervised swimming season runs roughly late June to early September, based on recent listings. Outside that window, treat it as a shore walk, not a swim.

Next door is Hallim Park, a garden park built on old lava fields, with palm gardens, a bonsai garden, and two lava caves you walk through underground. Give it 1.5 to 2 hours. Admission runs about 12,000 to 15,000 KRW for an adult, based on recent listings. The park is the weather-proof part of this day: if the wind picks up on the beach, the palm garden and the caves still work.

{{< figure src="images/photos/jeju-west-05.webp" alt="Gardens inside Hallim Park in western Jeju" caption="Gardens inside Hallim Park in western Jeju. Photo: Grapesurgeon, CC BY-SA 4.0" >}}

> **Tip:** Hyeopjae and Geumneung beaches sit next to each other, and Geumneung is the quieter of the two. If Hyeopjae is busy, walk 10 minutes east and you get the same sand with fewer people.

## After lunch: tea fields at O'sulloc

Drive or take the bus inland to O'sulloc Tea Museum, about 30 to 40 minutes from Hyeopjae. The museum sits beside wide rows of green tea, and the building itself is worth a slow lap even if you skip the exhibits. The cafe serves green tea lattes, matcha soft-serve, and tea desserts, roughly 5,000 to 9,000 KRW each, based on recent listings.

Two things to know before you go. First, the tea fields are a working farm, so stay on the paths and do not walk into the rows for photos. Second, the museum runs booked tea programs that sell out, so if you want one, book ahead and do not count on a walk-in spot.

## Which cafe stop is right for you?

Aewol has more cafes than any other stretch of coast on Jeju, and they split into two kinds. Here is how to pick.

| Cafe type | What it is like | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Big ocean-view cafe | Two or three floors, glass walls, a menu of lattes and cakes | Sunset photos and a long sit |
| Small roastery | Counter seats, hand drip, beans to buy | Coffee people and a quiet hour |
| Bakery cafe | Bread and pastries first, coffee second | A late lunch that is not a restaurant |

Prices run about 6,000 to 9,000 KRW for a latte and 7,000 to 12,000 KRW for a cake slice, based on recent listings. The big cafes fill up on weekend afternoons, so if you want a window seat, arrive before 15:00 or after 17:00.

## Sunset on Saebyeol Oreum

Saebyeol Oreum is a low volcanic cone, and it is the sunset stop on this route. The walk up takes about 20 to 30 minutes on a clear path, and the top gives you a wide view over the fields and the sea. It is not a hard climb, but wear proper shoes: the path is dirt and loose stone in places.

{{< figure src="images/photos/jeju-west-03.webp" alt="Saebyeol Oreum, a low volcanic cone in western Jeju" caption="Saebyeol Oreum, a low volcanic cone in western Jeju. Photo: M Min, CC BY 3.0" >}}

Time it so you reach the top about 30 minutes before sunset. Bring a layer: the wind on the cone is stronger than it feels at the car park, and it cools fast after the sun drops. If the sky is cloudy, skip the climb and go straight to Aewol instead. A grey sunset from a hill is not better than a good coffee by the sea.

## Finish on the Aewol cafe street

End the day on the Handam Coastal Trail at Aewol, a flat lava-rock walk along the water lined with cafes. Walk for 30 to 40 minutes, pick a cafe, and sit. This is the part of the day to stop planning. The trail is the destination, and the sunset, the seat, and the light are never guaranteed, so enjoy whichever version you get.
