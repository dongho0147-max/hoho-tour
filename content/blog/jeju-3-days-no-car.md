You can do Jeju in three days without renting a car, and you can enjoy it. The trick is to stop trying to cover the whole island and instead base yourself in Jeju City, use the bus for the east coast, and spend your last day on one walk instead of three rushed stops.

This guide was put together from recent listings, reviews, and official transport pages, checked November 2026. Bus numbers and fares change, so treat the numbers below as ranges and check the day before you travel. This route is built for a first-time visitor who would rather ride the bus than argue with a rental desk.

## 🚌 How do you get around Jeju without a car?

Jeju has three useful bus systems, and you only need to learn two of them. City buses (blue) run around Jeju City and the airport. Trunk buses (red, numbers in the 100s and 200s) run between towns, and they are the ones that take you east to Hamdeok and Seongsan. The third group is the green village buses, which are slow and local, and you can ignore them for this trip.

Buy a T-money card at a convenience store at the airport and top it up with about {{< pr "30,000 KRW" >}}. A city bus ride costs around 1,150 to {{< pr "1,300 KRW" >}} with the card, and a trunk ride to the east runs about 2,000 to {{< pr "4,000 KRW" >}} depending on distance. You tap when you board and tap again when you get off, and the fare is calculated for you.

> **🔥 Local tip:** Download Naver Map before you land and set it to English. It shows live bus times in Jeju, which Google Maps does not do well. Check the last bus home before you leave for the day, because east-coast buses thin out after 19:00.

If a full bus day sounds like too much planning, a small-group [day tour](https://www.klook.com/en-US/activity/189127-jeju-east-west-south-shared-day-tour/?aid=145455) does the same east-coast loop with hotel pickup, and it is the simplest fix for day 2.

## 🌅 Day 1: Jeju City on foot and by bus

Land, drop your bags, and stay near the old downtown or the airport area. Both work, but old downtown puts you closer to the market and the evening food streets, while the airport area is quieter. From the airport, bus 100 or 200 reaches downtown in about 20 to 30 minutes, depending on traffic.

Spend the afternoon walking. Start at Dongmun Traditional Market, which is the loud, crowded heart of the city. Go hungry and graze: fried chicken skewers, tangerine juice, and the small fried snacks the stalls sell by weight. Prices run about 3,000 to {{< pr "8,000 KRW" >}} per snack, and a full grazing lunch lands around 12,000 to {{< pr "18,000 KRW" >}} per person.

> **🔥 Local tip:** The day market runs 08:00 to 21:00 and the night market 18:00 to 24:00, and the 19:00 to 21:00 night-market rush is the busiest stretch. Go before 19:00 if you want the stalls quieter. Based on recent listings/reviews, checked November 2026.

{{< figure src="images/photos/jeju-05.webp" alt="Stalls inside Dongmun Traditional Market in Jeju City" caption="The night-market rush is 19:00 to 21:00. Go before 19:00 for quieter stalls. Photo: Grapesurgeon, CC BY-SA 4.0" >}}

After the market, walk to Yongduam Rock on the coast, about 30 minutes on foot or one short bus ride away. It is a small dragon-shaped rock in the sea, and the point is the walk there along the water, not the rock itself. Come back through the black-pork streets near the old city for dinner. Black pork barbecue runs about 15,000 to {{< pr "25,000 KRW" >}} per person, and most places have picture menus.

> **🔥 Local tip:** The Nohyeong black-pork street near the airport has plenty of walk-in spots, but the popular ones fill up for the 19:00 to 21:00 dinner rush. Book ahead through Naver or Catchtable if you have your heart set on one place. Based on recent listings/reviews, checked November 2026.

## 🏖️ Day 2: the east coast by bus

Day 2 is the big one, and the bus makes it work if you start early. Take trunk bus 701 or 702 from Jeju City toward Hamdeok Beach first, about 40 to 50 minutes. Hamdeok is the prettiest easy beach on the north coast, with clear water and white sand, and it is calm before the afternoon wind.

{{< figure src="images/photos/jeju-04.webp" alt="Hamdeok Beach in Jocheon with clear water and white sand" caption="Trunk bus 701 or 702 takes 40 to 50 minutes. The beach is calm before the afternoon wind. Photo: Hong Da Hyeon, CC0" >}}

From Hamdeok, stay on the eastbound trunk bus toward Seongsan. The ride takes another hour or so, and the bus stops at Seongeup Folk Village on the way. Get off there for an hour. The village is a small cluster of thatched stone houses, and it is free to walk through. It is also the best place on this route to see what a Jeju farmhouse actually looked like.

{{< figure src="images/photos/jeju-06.webp" alt="Thatched stone houses at Seongeup Folk Village" caption="Get off the bus here for an hour. The village is free to walk through. Photo: Seefooddiet, CC BY-SA 4.0" >}}

End the day at Seongsan Ilchulbong, the tuff cone on the eastern tip. The stair climb to the top takes 30 to 40 minutes, and the crater view is worth it. Ticket sales stop an hour before closing, and the site closes on the first Monday of each month, so check the day before. The last bus back to Jeju City leaves Seongsan in the early evening, so plan to be on it. If that timing feels tight, a private car with a driver covers the same east loop without the bus timetable.

{{< figure src="images/photos/jeju-07.webp" alt="Seongsan Ilchulbong rising over Seongsan village and the sea" caption="The peak closes on the first Monday of each month, and ticket sales stop an hour before closing. Catch the last bus back. Photo: Justin De La Ornell, CC BY 2.0" >}}

> **🔥 Local tip:** Seongsan closes on the first Monday of every month, and if that Monday is a public holiday it closes the next day instead, so check before you set day 2 in stone. Express buses 111 and 101 also run between the airport or central Jeju City and Seongsan, which can be faster than the trunk bus. Based on recent listings/reviews, checked November 2026.

## ⛰️ Day 3: one mountain, or one coastal walk

Day 3 is a choice, and you should pick one. Option A is Hallasan, the mountain in the middle of the island. Bus 240 runs from Jeju City to the Eorimok trail entrance in about 50 minutes. The Eorimok trail is a steady uphill walk of about 5 km each way, and most people take 4 to 5 hours return. It does not reach the summit, but it gives you the forest and the high meadow, which is the point for most walkers.

Option B is an Olle Trail section, and it is the better pick if your legs are tired from day 2. The Olle trails are marked coastal walking routes, and the blue and orange ribbons are the markers you follow. Pick a flat section near Jeju City or Hamdeok, walk for 2 to 3 hours, and stop at a cafe when you feel like it. It is a walk, not a hike, and that is the appeal.

## 🛏️ Where should you stay?

Stay in Jeju City for all three nights. Moving hotels eats half a day on an island where the bus is your transport, and Jeju City has the best bus connections in every direction. Pick a place within a 10-minute walk of a trunk bus stop, and check that before you book, not after.

Budget guesthouses near the old downtown run about 30,000 to {{< pr "60,000 KRW" >}} a night. Mid-range hotels near the airport and downtown run about 80,000 to {{< pr "150,000 KRW" >}}. Prices move a lot with weekends and holidays, so the range matters more than any single number.
