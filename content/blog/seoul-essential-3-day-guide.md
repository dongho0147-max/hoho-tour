Three days is the sweet spot for a first trip to Seoul. One day is a blur. Five days lets you add Busan or Jeju. Three days covers the palace and the old streets, with time left over for the food alleys and the river, and you still get an evening to yourself.

I live in Seoul, and every stop below is a place I actually go. Prices are ranges in Korean won, last checked November 2026. They move a little year to year, so treat the big ones as a shape, not a promise.

## Before you go

### Getting from Incheon Airport to Seoul

- **AREX Express train** to Seoul Station: about 45 minutes, roughly 11,000 KRW. Fastest and simplest. Buy at the airport station.
- **AREX all-stop train**: cheaper (around 4,000-5,000 KRW), about an hour, stops at Hongik University - handy if you stay in Hongdae.
- **Airport limousine bus**: about 16,000-17,000 KRW, 60-90 minutes depending on traffic. Best if your hotel is near a bus stop and you have big luggage.
- **Taxi**: 60,000-80,000 KRW to central Seoul, more late at night. Only worth it for 3-4 people with luggage.

If you want the train ticket sorted before you land, the express is easy to book ahead.

### Money, SIM, and the subway card

- **Get a T-money card** at any convenience store at the airport (about 5,000 KRW for the card, then top up). It works on the subway, buses, and even at convenience stores. Tap in, tap out.
- **Cash vs card:** Cards work almost everywhere in Seoul, including street stalls in Myeongdong. Keep 20,000-30,000 KRW in cash for small markets.
- **eSIM or SIM:** Buy an eSIM before you fly, or a tourist SIM at the airport. You will use maps constantly - do not rely on public Wi-Fi.
- **No tipping.** It is not expected in Korea. Good service is the default.
- **Language:** English works in tourist areas, hotels, and most restaurants with picture menus. A translation app covers the rest.

### Where to stay

For a first trip, stay near a subway station in one of these three areas. Myeongdong is the most central for sightseeing, and the best for food and shopping. It is crowded, but you can walk to a lot. Hongdae is younger and cheaper, with the best nightlife and cafes, and the AREX all-stop train goes straight there from the airport. Insadong and Jongno are quiet and traditional, closest to the palaces, and best if you want early mornings at Gyeongbokgung.

## Day 1: Palaces, hanok streets, and old Seoul

Start early. The palaces are best before the tour buses arrive.

### Morning: Gyeongbokgung Palace

Seoul's main royal palace, built in 1395. Come for the 10:00 or 14:00 Royal Guard Changing Ceremony at Gwanghwamun Gate. It lasts about 20 minutes and is free to watch from the square.

Admission runs about 3,000 KRW for adults. Wear a hanbok and entry is free. Rentals from shops near the palace run roughly 15,000-30,000 KRW for a few hours, and your photos will look far better for it. Give the palace 2 hours. Do not rush the throne hall and the back gardens.

The honest downside: the courtyard gets packed by 11, and the ceremony draws a crowd three deep. Come at 10 and you get the good light and the space. Subway: Gyeongbokgung Station, Line 3, Exit 5.

If you want the hanbok sorted before you go, rentals near the palace are easy to book ahead.

### Lunch: Tongin Market

A 10-minute walk from the palace. This old market runs a lunchbox system. Buy coins at the entrance, then fill a tray with small dishes from the stalls. Expect about 10,000-15,000 KRW per person. It is where office workers eat, which is usually a good sign.

It closes early in the afternoon, and the coin counter gets a line at noon. Go straight from the palace and you will miss the worst of it.

### Afternoon: Bukchon Hanok Village and Insadong

Bukchon is the hillside neighborhood of traditional wooden houses between the two palaces. Walk the famous photo alley, but keep your voice down. People live here, and the village now limits tourist hours in the residential lanes. About 1 hour is enough, and that is plenty. The roofs are the view. The quiet is part of it.

{{< collage "images/photos/seoul-03.webp|Bukchon at blue hour|Basile Morin, CC BY-SA 4.0" "images/photos/seoul-26.webp|Buk drum at Gyeongbokgung|IssamBarhoumi, CC BY-SA 4.0" >}}

Insadong sits just south of Bukchon. It is the traditional craft and tea street, good for souvenirs that are not airport junk: ceramics, paper, tea. Stop at a traditional tea house for a break. Your feet will want one by then.

### Evening: Cheonggyecheon Stream

Walk the restored stream that runs through downtown. It is a 20-minute evening stroll, lit up at night, and free. It is the calmest place in central Seoul after a long day.

## Day 2: Hongdae, the Han River, and N Seoul Tower

### Morning: Hongdae

Seoul's university district. Mornings are quiet, which makes them good for cafes and for the street performances that start in the afternoon. Browse the small designer shops. If your trip includes a Saturday, the Hongdae Free Market is worth a slow lap.

Subway: Hongik University Station, Line 2 / AREX, Exit 9.

### Afternoon: the Han River

Take the subway to Yeouido Hangang Park or Banpo Hangang Park. Rent a bike for about 3,000 KRW per hour, or just walk the riverside path. In the evening, Banpo Bridge runs its rainbow fountain show. Check the season schedule before you go - it does not run in winter.

Buy instant noodles and a boiled egg at the park convenience store and eat them on the riverbank. It is a small ritual for Seoulites, and it costs less than 5,000 KRW.

### Evening: N Seoul Tower

Take the cable car up from Myeongdong, or the bus from Itaewon. The observatory costs around 20,000 KRW. The outdoor terrace and the city view are free. Come at sunset and stay for the lights.

One warning: the cable car line at sunset can eat 40 minutes. The bus from Itaewon is slower but you stay warm, and the plaza view is almost as good as the paid observatory. It costs nothing.

## Day 3: Food streets, markets, and shopping

### Morning: Gwangjang Market

One of Seoul's oldest markets, famous for food. Bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes), mayak gimbap (mini seaweed rolls), and knife-cut noodles. Go hungry, bring cash, and point at what looks good. Budget 10,000-15,000 KRW for a full breakfast.

Subway: Jongno 5-ga Station, Line 1, Exit 8.

{{< figure src="images/photos/seoul-13.webp" alt="Customers eating noodles and dumplings at a Gwangjang Market food stall" caption="A noodle stall at Gwangjang Market, lunch eaten shoulder to shoulder. Photo: kallerna, CC BY-SA 4.0" >}}

### Afternoon: Myeongdong food street

Myeongdong is a shopping district first and a food street second, but the street food is the point for most first-timers. Grilled lobster tails, egg bread, tteokbokki, and Korean fried chicken cups. Prices run 3,000-10,000 KRW per item. Share, so you can try more.

After eating, shop. Skincare and cosmetics are the main draw, and most shops offer tax refunds for tourists. Ask at the counter and bring your passport.

{{< figure src="images/photos/seoul-06.webp" alt="Myeongdong street at night with neon signs, taxis, and a bus" caption="Myeongdong at night, neon and food stalls. Photo: Tristan Surtel, CC BY-SA 4.0" >}}

### Evening: Dongdaemun or a farewell dinner

Dongdaemun Design Plaza is the curved silver building worth seeing at night. The shopping malls around it stay open late. For a farewell dinner, Korean BBQ runs 15,000-30,000 KRW per person. A fried chicken and beer night costs less and is just as local. If you want one last real experience, eat at a place with no English menu. Point, smile, and let the staff guide you.

## Where to eat on this route

Three stops cover most of the eating in this guide, and none of them needs a reservation.

- **Tongin Market lunchbox, near Gyeongbokgung.** Coins at the entrance, a tray of small dishes from the stalls. About 10,000-15,000 KRW per person. Go before noon.
- **Gwangjang Market stalls, Jongno 5-ga.** Bindaetteok runs roughly 5,000-8,000 KRW. A tray of mayak gimbap costs a few thousand won more. Cash helps.
- **Myeongdong street food.** Items run 3,000-10,000 KRW each. Two people sharing can taste five things and still spend less than one restaurant dinner.

## If you have a 4th day: pick one day trip

- **Gyeongju (day trip by KTX + bus, 2 hours each way):** Korea's ancient capital. Tombs, temples, and the quietest big-historic-city feel in the country. Best for history lovers.
- **DMZ tour (half day, book a licensed tour in advance):** the border zone with North Korea. You cannot go alone - book through a licensed operator.
- **Nami Island + Garden of Morning Calm (full day):** tree-lined island walks and gardens. Best in autumn or spring.

For the DMZ, a licensed tour is the only way in, and booking ahead is not optional.

## Practical tips that save real time

- **Subway first, taxi second.** Seoul's subway runs 05:30 to about midnight and covers almost everything in this guide. A taxi is only faster late at night.
- **Naver Map, not Google Maps.** Google Maps works poorly for walking and transit directions in Korea. Install Naver Map (English available) before you arrive.
- **Rush hour is real.** Avoid the subway 08:00-09:30 and 18:00-19:30 if you can.
- **Restaurants close between lunch and dinner.** Many smaller places shut 15:00-17:00. Plan lunch before 14:00.
- **Carry your passport for tax refunds.** Most big shops refund VAT on purchases over 30,000 KRW - ask at the counter.

## FAQ

**Is 3 days enough for Seoul?**
Yes, for the essentials in this guide. You will not see everything, but you will see the city properly: palace, old town, river, food, and one great view.

**What is the best month to visit Seoul?**
April-May (cherry blossoms, mild weather) and October-November (autumn leaves, clear skies). Summer is hot and humid. Winter is cold but clear and cheaper.

**Is Seoul expensive?**
A mid-range day runs about 80,000-120,000 KRW per person including a hotel share, food, transport, and one paid attraction. Street food and markets keep it well below that if you want.

**Is Seoul safe at night?**
Seoul is one of the safest large cities in the world for tourists, including solo travelers at night. Normal common sense applies, as anywhere.
