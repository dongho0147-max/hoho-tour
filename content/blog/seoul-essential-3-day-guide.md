Three days is the sweet spot for a first trip to Seoul. One day is a blur,
five days lets you add Busan or Jeju - but three days is enough to see the
palace, the old neighborhoods, the food streets, and the river, without
spending your whole trip on the subway.

This guide is written by someone who lives in Seoul. Every stop below is a
place we have actually been, on the subway lines we actually ride. Prices are
approximate ranges in Korean won (KRW), last checked November 2026.

## Before you go

### How many days do you need in Seoul?

Three full days covers the essentials below. Add a fourth day if you want a
day trip (Gyeongju, the DMZ, or Nami Island), and a fifth if you want to slow
down and add a second food or shopping day. Less than two days, and you will
mostly see the airport express.

### Getting from Incheon Airport to Seoul

- **AREX Express train** to Seoul Station: about 45 minutes, roughly
  11,000 KRW. Fastest and simplest. Buy at the airport station.
- **AREX all-stop train**: cheaper (around 4,000-5,000 KRW), about an hour,
  stops at Hongik University - handy if you stay in Hongdae.
- **Airport limousine bus**: about 16,000-17,000 KRW, 60-90 minutes
  depending on traffic. Best if your hotel is near a bus stop and you have
  big luggage.
- **Taxi**: 60,000-80,000 KRW to central Seoul, more late at night. Only
  worth it for 3-4 people with luggage.

### Money, SIM, and the subway card

- **Get a T-money card** at any convenience store at the airport (about
  5,000 KRW for the card, then top up). It works on the subway, buses, and
  even at convenience stores. Tap in, tap out.
- **Cash vs card:** Cards work almost everywhere in Seoul, including street
  stalls in Myeongdong. Keep 20,000-30,000 KRW in cash for small markets.
- **eSIM or SIM:** Buy an eSIM before you fly, or a tourist SIM at the
  airport. You will use maps constantly - do not rely on public Wi-Fi.
- **No tipping.** It is not expected in Korea. Good service is the default.
- **Language:** English works in tourist areas, hotels, and most
  restaurants with picture menus. A translation app covers the rest.

### Where to stay

For a first trip, stay near a subway station in one of these three areas:

- **Myeongdong** - most central for sightseeing, best for food and shopping.
  Crowded, but you can walk to a lot.
- **Hongdae** - younger, cheaper, best nightlife and cafes. AREX all-stop
  train goes straight here from the airport.
- **Insadong / Jongno** - quiet, traditional, closest to the palaces. Best
  if you want early mornings at Gyeongbokgung.

## Day 1: Palaces, hanok streets, and old Seoul

Start early. The palaces are best before the tour buses arrive.

### Morning: Gyeongbokgung Palace

Seoul's main royal palace, built in 1395. Come for the 10:00 or 14:00
Royal Guard Changing Ceremony at Gwanghwamun Gate - it lasts about 20
minutes and is free to watch from the square.

- **Admission:** about 3,000 KRW for adults.
- **Free entry trick:** wear a hanbok (rented from shops near the palace,
  roughly 15,000-30,000 KRW for a few hours) and entry is free - and your
  photos will look far better.
- **Time needed:** 2 hours. Do not rush the back gardens and the throne
  hall.
- **Subway:** Gyeongbokgung Station, Line 3, Exit 5.

### Lunch: Tongin Market

A 10-minute walk from the palace. This old market runs a lunchbox system:
buy coins at the entrance, fill a tray with small dishes from the stalls.
Expect about 10,000-15,000 KRW per person. It is where office workers eat,
which is usually a good sign.

### Afternoon: Bukchon Hanok Village and Insadong

- **Bukchon Hanok Village:** the hillside neighborhood of traditional
  Korean wooden houses (hanok) between the two palaces. Walk the famous
  photo alley, but keep your voice down - people live here, and the village
  now limits tourist hours in residential lanes. About 1 hour is enough.
- **Insadong:** the traditional craft and tea street just south of Bukchon.
  Good for souvenirs that are not airport junk: ceramics, paper, tea.
  Stop at a traditional tea house for a break.

> **Photo spot:** the Bukchon photo alley looking down the tiled roofs
> toward the modern city. *[Photo to be added - our own photo.]*

### Evening: Cheonggyecheon Stream

Walk the restored stream that runs through downtown - a 20-minute evening
stroll, lit up at night, and free. It is the calmest place in central Seoul
after a long day.

## Day 2: Hongdae, the Han River, and N Seoul Tower

### Morning: Hongdae

Seoul's university district. Mornings are quiet - good for cafes and the
free street performances that start in the afternoon. Browse the small
designer shops and the Hongdae Free Market on Saturdays if your trip
includes one.

- **Subway:** Hongik University Station, Line 2 / AREX, Exit 9.

### Afternoon: the Han River

Take the subway to Yeouido Hangang Park or Banpo Hangang Park. Rent a bike
(about 3,000 KRW per hour), or just walk the riverside path. In the evening,
Banpo Bridge runs its rainbow fountain show (check the season schedule -
it does not run in winter).

- **Ramyeon by the river:** buy instant noodles and a boiled egg at the
  park convenience store and eat them on the riverbank. It is a small
  ritual for Seoulites, and it costs less than 5,000 KRW.

> **Photo spot:** Banpo Bridge rainbow fountain at dusk. *[Photo to be
> added - our own photo.]*

### Evening: N Seoul Tower

Take the cable car up from Myeongdong, or the bus from Itaewon. The
observatory costs around 20,000 KRW; the outdoor terrace and the city view
are free. Come at sunset and stay for the lights.

- **Budget tip:** the view from the tower plaza is almost as good as the
  paid observatory, and it costs nothing.

## Day 3: Food streets, markets, and shopping

### Morning: Gwangjang Market

One of Seoul's oldest markets, famous for food: bindaetteok (mung bean
pancakes), mayak gimbap (mini seaweed rolls), and knife-cut noodles. Go
hungry, bring cash, and point at what looks good. Budget 10,000-15,000 KRW
for a full breakfast.

- **Subway:** Jongno 5-ga Station, Line 1, Exit 8.

### Afternoon: Myeongdong food street

Myeongdong is a shopping district first and a food street second, but the
street food is the point for most first-timers: grilled lobster tails,
egg bread, tteokbokki, and Korean fried chicken cups. Prices run
3,000-10,000 KRW per item - share, so you can try more.

After eating, shop: skincare and cosmetics are the main draw, and most
shops offer tax refunds for tourists (ask at the counter, bring your
passport).

> **Photo spot:** Myeongdong main street at night, neon and food stalls.

### Evening: Dongdaemun or a farewell dinner

- **Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP):** the curved silver building is worth
  seeing at night. The shopping malls around it stay open late.
- **Farewell dinner:** Korean BBQ (expect 15,000-30,000 KRW per person) or
  a proper fried chicken and beer night. If you want one last local
  experience, eat at a place with no English menu - point, smile, and let
  the staff guide you.

## If you have a 4th day: pick one day trip

- **Gyeongju (day trip by KTX + bus, 2 hours each way):** Korea's ancient
  capital - tombs, temples, and the quietest big-historic-city feel in the
  country. Best for history lovers.
- **DMZ tour (half day, book a licensed tour in advance):** the border zone
  with North Korea. You cannot go alone - book through a licensed operator.
- **Nami Island + Garden of Morning Calm (full day):** tree-lined island
  walks and gardens. Best in autumn or spring.

## Practical tips that save real time

- **Subway first, taxi second.** Seoul's subway runs 05:30 to about
  midnight and covers almost everything in this guide. A taxi is only
  faster late at night.
- **Naver Map, not Google Maps.** Google Maps works poorly for walking and
  transit directions in Korea. Install Naver Map (English available) before
  you arrive.
- **Rush hour is real.** Avoid the subway 08:00-09:30 and 18:00-19:30 if
  you can.
- **Restaurants close between lunch and dinner.** Many smaller places shut
  15:00-17:00. Plan lunch before 14:00.
- **Carry your passport for tax refunds.** Most big shops refund VAT on
  purchases over 30,000 KRW - ask at the counter.

## FAQ

**Is 3 days enough for Seoul?**
Yes, for the essentials in this guide. You will not see everything, but
you will see the city properly - palace, old town, river, food, and one
great view.

**What is the best month to visit Seoul?**
April-May (cherry blossoms, mild weather) and October-November (autumn
leaves, clear skies). Summer is hot and humid; winter is cold but clear
and cheaper.

**Is Seoul expensive?**
A mid-range day runs about 80,000-120,000 KRW per person including a hotel
share, food, transport, and one paid attraction. Street food and markets
keep it well below that if you want.

**Is Seoul safe at night?**
Seoul is one of the safest large cities in the world for tourists, including
solo travelers at night. Normal common sense applies, as anywhere.
