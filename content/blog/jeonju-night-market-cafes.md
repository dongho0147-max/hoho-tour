Jeonju by day is a history walk. Jeonju by night is a food crawl. The day-trippers leave, the lanterns come on, the market stalls fire up their grills, and the village turns into the kind of place where you eat something every twenty minutes without planning to. If you only see Jeonju in daylight, you have seen half of it.

This guide is written from research, not from a single visit. Routes, times, and prices below come from recent listings and reviews, checked November 2026. Treat prices as ranges, not promises, and check the night market schedule before you go, since it runs on specific evenings.

## 🌇 Where does the evening start?

Start at Omokdae, the small pavilion on the hill at the edge of the [Hanok Village](https://www.klook.com/en-US/destination/p50001575-jeonju-hanok-village/?aid=145455). Go up before sunset, not after. The climb is short, the view over the rooftops is the best in town, and you can watch the light change over the village while the lanterns below switch on one by one. Bring a light jacket, because the hill catches the wind after dark.

From Omokdae, walk down into the village slowly. The main streets are quieter now, the hanok guesthouses glow warm from the inside, and the cafes put their tables out. This is the best hour for photos, and it is also the best hour for doing nothing in particular. Sit on a bench. Watch the village settle.

## 🌮 What is the Nambu Market night market actually like?

Nambu Market is the old market at the edge of the village, and on Friday and Saturday evenings it turns into a night market with food stalls, live music, and crowds of locals. Based on recent listings, the night market runs on Friday and Saturday evenings, roughly from early evening until late, but the exact hours shift by season, so check the current schedule before you plan around it.

The food is the point. Stalls sell grilled skewers, fried snacks, rice cakes, and drinks, and most items run {{< pr "3,000-8,000 KRW" >}} each based on recent listings. The method is simple: walk the whole market once without buying anything, then go back for the three things you actually want. Eat standing up, share everything, and keep moving. If you sit down for a full meal first, you will be too full to enjoy the stalls, and the stalls are the reason you came.

> **🔥 Local tip:** Go to the night market hungry and bring cash in small bills. Many stalls are cash-only, the card readers are slow, and the queue behind you will not wait while you fumble with an app. The stalls are fully stocked and the lines shortest before 19:00, and the market starts to thin out after 21:00, so eat in the first hour. The mung bean pancakes at the Yanggane Nokdujeon stall run about {{< pr "5,000 KRW" >}}, and the night market closes around 22:00 on Friday and 23:00 on Saturday. Based on recent listings/reviews, checked November 2026.

## 🍶 What to order after the market

Makgeolli. Jeonju is one of the best cities in Korea for it, and the local style comes with a table full of side dishes. Order a kettle of makgeolli and the food keeps coming: pancakes, stews, vegetables, and small plates you did not ask for and will finish anyway. A kettle for two with side dishes usually runs {{< pr "20,000-40,000 KRW" >}} based on recent listings, depending on the place. The bars near the village and on Gaekridan-gil stay open late, and the older the room looks, the better the food tends to be.

> **🔥 Local tip:** Order one kettle and look at the table before you order a second. One kettle arrives with 15 to 25 small side dishes, so a second kettle often goes to waste. The makgeolli alleys around Samcheon-dong and Seosin-dong work the same way, so pick the place with a line of locals outside rather than the one with the biggest sign. If you do not drink, the same restaurants serve the food without the makgeolli, and the night market has plenty of non-alcoholic options too. Nobody will mind. Jeonju is a food city first and a drinking city second.

## 🌙 Where do you go when the market closes?

Walk to the river. The Jeonju River runs past Nambu Market, and the riverside path is quiet and well lit after dark. It is the right place to walk off dinner, and it is much calmer than the village streets. Ten minutes there resets the evening, and then you can decide whether you want a second round or a bed.

The second round is Gaekridan-gil. The cafe street near the old Gaeksa stays open later than the village, and the bars there are small and friendly. Pick one, order one drink, and stop. The evening works best when it ends before midnight, because the morning in the village is worth waking up for.

## ☕ What about cafes at night?

Cafes in the Hanok Village mostly close between 21:00 and 22:00, based on recent listings, so do not plan a midnight coffee in the village itself. Gaekridan-gil keeps later hours, and a few dessert cafes there serve drinks until late. If you want a nightcap, go to Gaekridan-gil. If you want a quiet tea before bed, buy it earlier in the day and drink it at your guesthouse.

If you are staying overnight, a hanok guesthouse in the village is the move. Prices run roughly {{< pr "50,000-120,000 KRW" >}} a night based on recent listings. Book ahead on weekends, because the good ones fill up first.

## 🚫 Skip this if...

Skip the night market if you are in Jeonju on a weekday, because the big night market is a Friday and Saturday thing, and a Tuesday evening in the village is quiet. That is not a bad thing, but it is a different evening: cafes, a walk, and an early night. Skip the makgeolli crawl if you have an early KTX the next morning, because Jeonju makgeolli is stronger than it tastes and the side dishes keep you eating longer than you planned. And skip the late-night bar round if you want the morning light in the village, because you cannot have both.
