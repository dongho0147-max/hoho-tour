The best meals in Seoul do not come with a view or a tasting menu. They come in a brass bowl at seven in the morning, or on a tray with small side dishes at noon, and they cost less than a movie ticket. The restaurants below have been open for decades. Some of them predate the subway lines that take you there.

This guide picks five. Two soup houses that open before most cafes. One chicken soup house by the palace. One small baekban diner, and one barbecue room for the evening. You could eat at all five in two days without rushing. Prices below are in Korean won, checked November 2026.

A few things to know before you start. Many of these places close for a break in the middle of the afternoon, usually around 15:00 to 16:30, and a couple of them shut on Sundays. The soup houses also close when the pot runs out, which can happen well before the posted closing time. Go early, check the hours on a map app the morning you go, and bring a card as well as some cash.

## 🍜 Why start at Imun Seolnongtang, the oldest bowl in Korea?

Imun Seolnongtang opened in 1904, and it is often called the oldest restaurant still operating in Korea. It sits in a side street off Ujeongguk-ro in Jongno, a five-minute walk from Jonggak Station, Exit 3. The MICHELIN Guide lists it, and the Seoul government lists it as a Seoul Future Heritage site. The room is plain. There is nothing to look at except the pot, and that is the point.

The broth is ox leg bones and knuckle simmered for 16 to 17 hours in cast-iron pots, skimmed until it turns milky but never greasy. A regular seolleongtang is 15,000 KRW. The special, with more meat, is 18,000 KRW, and the doganitang made with knee cartilage is 18,000 KRW too. The soup arrives unsalted. You season it yourself at the table with salt and pepper, then add the chopped scallions. It is mild by design. If you want heat, this is not your bowl, and no amount of pepper fixes that.

It opens at 08:00. Monday to Saturday it runs to 21:00, Sunday to 20:00, with a break from 15:00 to 16:30 every day. One item only shows up from 10:30 onward, the mana, or beef spleen, at 16,000 KRW. Go for breakfast, when the room is quiet and the broth is at its freshest, and do not plan anything for the 15:00 break. The one real downside is that the place is so well known now that lunch can fill up with tour groups. Early morning is still the locals' hour.

> **🔥 Local tip:** The room turns over fast, so most visits report no wait, but the weekday lunch peak around 11:30 to 13:30 can draw a short line. Arrive before 11:30 or after 13:30 to walk straight in. And if you came for the mana, remember it is only served from 10:30 onward, so an 08:00 breakfast will not include it. ## 🥣 What makes Hadongkwan different? One soup, three sizes, gone by afternoon

Hadongkwan has served gomtang since 1939, and it has served almost nothing else since. Gomtang is the clearer cousin of seolleongtang, made from brisket and tripe rather than bones, with the rice already in the bowl. You pay at the counter as you come in. The menu is one soup in three sizes, plus a plate of sliced boiled beef if you want it.

A regular bowl is 19,000 KRW as of November 2026, up from 18,000 KRW earlier in the year. The 25-gong, with more meat, is 25,000 KRW, and the 30-gong is 30,000 KRW. The names sound strange until you realize the number is the old price in hundreds of won, and the names simply never changed. There is only one side dish, a sharp kkakdugi of cubed radish kimchi, and that is all the bowl needs.

The hours are the famous part. Monday to Saturday, 07:00 to 16:00, closed Sundays and public holidays. The day ends when the pot does, and on a busy day that can be before 14:00. It sits at 12 Myeongdong 9-gil, about five minutes from Euljiro 1-ga Station or Myeongdong Station. Go before noon on a weekday. Come for dinner and the door will be shut. The price is the honest complaint here. At 19,000 KRW for soup, some visitors feel the bowl has outrun the experience. The 25-gong earns it better, and the regular bowl is still the cheapest way to eat 86 years of habit.

> **🔥 Local tip:** Eat Imun and Hadongkwan on separate mornings and compare them. The two soups look alike on paper and taste nothing alike in the bowl, and comparing them is the cheapest food education in the city.

> **🔥 Local tip:** Hadongkwan is the time-sensitive one: the day ends when the soup runs out, and on a busy day that can be before 14:00, so do not plan it as a late lunch. Pay at the entrance counter when you arrive, not at the table after eating, and taste the broth before adding salt. Based on recent listings/reviews, checked November 2026.

## 🐔 Where is the palace chicken soup? Tosokchon Samgyetang

Tosokchon sits in a cluster of hanok buildings in Seochon, a few minutes from Gyeongbokgung Station, Exit 2. It has been serving samgyetang, ginseng chicken soup, since 1983. The building does half the work before the food arrives: low tile roofs, dark wood, paper screen doors, a courtyard in the middle. You walk in from a palace visit and the whole day slows down.

The standard samgyetang is 20,000 KRW, a whole young chicken stuffed with rice and ginseng and simmered until the broth turns opaque. The black silkie chicken version is 25,000 KRW. There is an English menu that explains how to eat it, which helps, because the chicken falls apart and the broth is hotter than it looks. It is open every day from 10:00 to 22:00, last order 21:00, which makes it the most forgiving stop on this list.

The wait is the catch. At weekends the line runs 20 to 30 minutes, and on bok-nal, the hottest days of summer when everyone eats samgyetang, it can pass an hour. There is an annex across the alley, and on a weekday lunch it sometimes has a shorter line. About half the room is visitors on a weekend, and it feels like it. Go on a weekday, or go at 10:00 when the doors open, and the hanok is quiet enough to enjoy.

> **🔥 Local tip:** The shortest waits are right after the 10:00 opening or in the mid-afternoon between lunch and dinner. The line is long but moves fast because the room is big and the soup comes out quickly. Tosokchon takes no reservations for regular samgyetang, but it runs a remote waitlist on the CatchTable app, so register first and walk around the palace until you are called. ## 🍱 What is a baekban tray? Siu Sikdang in Seochon

Siu Sikdang is a small neighborhood diner at 18 Pilun-daero in Seochon, about six or seven minutes from Gyeongbokgung Station, Exit 2. It does home-style set meals, the kind of lunch office workers eat on a normal Tuesday. The grilled pork belly set is 12,000 KRW. The spicy stir-fried pork set is 10,000 KRW. Most of the menu sits around 10,000 KRW.

The pork set arrives as a full tray. Grilled pork belly comes with rice and seaweed soup, plus kimchi and small side dishes around the main. It is a lot of food for 12,000 KRW in a neighborhood where lunch usually runs 13,000 to 15,000 KRW. The room is bright and wood-toned, and it fills within 10 to 20 minutes of opening at 11:00. There is no English menu. Pointing works, and the staff are used to it.

It is open Monday to Saturday, 11:00 to 20:00, last order 19:30, and closed on Sundays. The room is small, so a group of four at noon will wait. The menu also lists a pollock-roe cream pasta at 10,000 KRW. Skip it. You are here for the pork tray, and the pasta is a distraction from a kitchen that knows exactly what it is good at.

## 🔥 How does barbecue work for a first-timer? Wangbijib in Myeongdong

Wangbijib is the barbecue exception in Myeongdong, a district where most grill houses aim at tour groups. The main shop is on the second floor at 26 Myeongdong 8ga-gil, a few minutes from Myeongdong Station, Exit 9. It has been open since 1976. The staff grill the meat at your table and cut it with scissors, which makes it an easy first barbecue even if you have never held the tongs.

Fresh pork belly is 19,000 KRW a portion. Marinated Korean pork is 24,000 KRW, and marinated beef short rib is 44,000 KRW. The best value is the lunch pork galbi set at 15,000 KRW, served from 11:30 to 15:00, and 17,000 KRW after that. The shop is open daily from 11:30 to 22:00, last order 21:15, and it takes reservations on Naver Booking. There is a one-person hanwoo set at 49,000 KRW, which is rare. Most barbecue rooms want two portions minimum, and solo diners get turned away.

{{< figure src="images/photos/seoul-43.webp" alt="Charcoal-grilled Korean beef on a mesh grill over glowing coals" caption="Charcoal-grilled hanwoo beef on the grill. Photo: lazy fri13th, CC BY 2.0" >}}

Dinner for two with drinks lands around 50,000 to 70,000 KRW, so it is not a cheap night. The stairs to the second floor are steep, and the room gets loud at peak hours. After 19:00 on a weekend, send one person ahead for a table. Order the pork belly for two, add a doenjang jjigae to finish, and let the staff do the grilling. That is the whole trick.
